Traces, Venice, 26-27 November 2026

Workshops

Three hands-on workshops run in parallel on Friday 27 November, 11:00-12:30, in the Sala polifunzionale Maria Rimoldi. They are held in person only. Each has up to 15 places, assigned in order of registration.

Managing Oral Heritage in the Born-Digital Era: FAIR-by-Design Approaches in Research Infrastructures

Monica Monachini, Elvira Mercatanti (CNR-ILC, CLARIN-IT)Abstract

This workshop explores the management, preservation, and reuse of oral heritage in the born-digital era, focusing on FAIR-by-Design approaches and the role of research infrastructures. An introduction to the oral data lifecycle will highlight how digital services support the creation of structured, accessible, and reusable resources, with particular attention to their documentation, long-term preservation, and responsible reuse practices, while addressing technical, methodological, and ethical challenges.

Through selected case studies, participants will explore methods for representing oral sources using shared standards and be introduced to a pilot platform developed within H2IOSC for archiving and accessing oral collections. The workshop will also introduce research infrastructure services for automatic transcription of oral recordings.

Finally, participants will take part in a hands-on activity using selected oral testimonies, gaining foundational knowledge of shared representation standards, archival description and preservation practices, and digital tools for managing and transcribing oral heritage.

Save, Image, Read: A Forensic Workflow for Born-Digital Literary Heritage

Mariangela Giglio (Ca’ Foscari University of Venice, University of Bologna)Abstract

Before a born-digital text can be read, its medium must be preserved without alteration, and the copy must be proven faithful. Copying the visible files is not enough: deleted documents, hidden earlier versions and file-system metadata survive only in a complete forensic image. In this hands-on workshop, participants will work in small groups on a 3.5-inch floppy disk containing Microsoft Word 5 for Macintosh drafts: they will write-protect the medium, create and verify a bit-level image, and document the acquisition. Opening the image, they will then discover what a simple copy would have lost. No prior technical experience is required.

“Save the Page”. Exploring Tools and Methods for Web Archiving

Adele Gorini, Alessia Del Bianco (University of Bologna)Abstract

The workshop introduces the field of web archiving, briefly exploring its history, development, and the main challenges involved in preserving online content. It will provide an overview of the international context, focusing on the different strategies, methods, and initiatives developed around the world.

Key concepts in digital preservation, such as reborn digital, complex objects, and significant properties, will be discussed, highlighting the value of archived web as a source for research.

Part of the workshop will be dedicated to practical demonstrations of tools and platforms for web archiving, exploration, and analysis, including the Internet Archive Wayback Machine and SolrWayback. Other tools, such as Webrecorder and Browsertrix, will also be introduced. The demonstrations will focus on their main features and possible uses, including full-text search, browsing and replaying archived web pages, data visualisation, and the analysis of historical web data.

The workshop aims to provide participants with both a theoretical and methodological introduction to web archiving and a first practical experience with tools for exploring, searching, and analysing archived web as both a source and an object of research.
